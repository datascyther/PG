# THREE POSTS — headline for the card, description in his own voice

Written as he would write them: first person, his rhythm, his blunt openers. Every claim is
either from the public record or a position he has already taken in his own writing.

## POST 1 — image `IMG_2546.jpg` (listening)

### Headline (on card)

**Every founder rehearses the pitch. The pause that follows it is the part nobody prepares for.**

### Description

I have heard the same pitch from different founders on the same day. The words change, the shape does not.

Every one of them had rehearsed the deck. None of them had rehearsed the pause after it.

That pause tells me more than the pitch does. I watch what he does when the number gets challenged, and whether he can say he does not know.

So what am I actually listening for in a pause?

The version of him that nobody prepared.

I spent two shooting days on the Born Unicorn Season 1 jury in New Delhi. Founders from five sectors came in front of us.

The pitch is what a founder prepared. The pause is what he actually is.

## POST 2 — image `IMG_2619.jpg` (the notebook)

### Headline (on card)

**The most useful note in the room is the question there was no time to ask out loud.**

### Description

I wrote through a good part of that afternoon. Notebook on my lap, pen in hand, phone face down on the table.

A pitch gives you one minute and a fixed set of questions. The useful note is the one I never get to ask out loud. The minute ends and the next founder is already walking in.

Born Unicorn Season 1 had taken 5,000 to 6,000 applications down to a Top 30, announced on 15 September. What reaches the table is a small number of people and a large amount of paper.

So what do you do with the question you did not get to ask?

You write it down. A score can be argued with later. A question you never wrote down is gone.

## POST 3 — image `IMG_2618.jpg` (two jurors, one writing, one talking)

### Headline (on card)

**A selection round is not a verdict. It is one person defending a founder while another tests the defence.**

### Description

One of us was writing. I was the one talking, pen still in my hand.

A verdict is quiet. It arrives after everyone has agreed. What happens at a table like this is louder and slower. I make the case for a founder, someone tests it, and whoever is writing keeps the sentence that survives.

So how does a room change its mind?

Usually after the pitch ends, when someone says the thing nobody had said yet.

Born Unicorn Season 1 ran two shooting days in New Delhi with six jurors and a guest. The panel mixed operators, investors and founders. The argument is how a room finds out whether it is right.
