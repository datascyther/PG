# CARD POST — "Panel Discussion: Defending Founders"

**Image:** `Panel Discussion_ Defending Founders.png` (the two-juror still, IMG_2618)
**Headline on the card:** A selection round is not a verdict. It is one person defending a
founder while another tests the defence…

---

## Description — first person, 135 words

I watched the same job happen twice at that table. One is finding out what the founder has, and the other is finding out what happens when it is taken away.

None of it looks dramatic. The person defending a founder is not being kind, and the person testing the defence is not being cruel.

I was the one talking, pen still in my hand. The juror beside me was writing, because whoever writes keeps the sentence that survives.

So what does a founder actually get judged on in that exchange?

Not the answers he prepared. The ones he has to improvise when someone pushes on the number he hoped nobody would push on.

A verdict takes a minute. The argument takes longer, and it is the only part that tells you anything.

---

## Why it is written this way

**The card ends on an ellipsis, so the body finishes the sentence.** A caps line closes a
thought; an ellipsis hands it over. That is why the first paragraph does not restate "one
person defending, another testing" — it says what both of them are actually for, which is the
thing the card leaves hanging.

**The question is the hinge, at 59% depth.** "So what does a founder actually get judged on in
that exchange?" turns the post from describing the process to naming what it produces. It is
not a restatement of the card, which makes no claim about judging.

**It matches the photograph exactly.** In this frame he is the one talking with a pen raised
and the juror beside him is the one writing. The first person follows the image: "I was the
one talking", never "I was taking notes". A description that contradicts its own picture is
the fastest way to look fabricated.

**The last line is a truth, not a request.** No question, no call to action, no "agree?".

**No new numbers, no named juror.** The second juror is unnamed on purpose — we do not have his
name, and a face is never named from a photograph.
