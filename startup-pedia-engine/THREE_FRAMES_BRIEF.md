# THE THREE FRAMES — what they are worth, and what to do with them

Written 25 October 2026, for the founder's office. Answers one question: three photos from
the Born Unicorn set have landed, the seniors want more samples, and nobody is sure what
can honestly be said.

---

## 1. The short answer

The three frames are not three photos. They are **three parts of one job**, shot on one
day, and that is why they make three posts and not one.

Read in capture order they run: **09:56 listening → 12:11 writing → 12:11 speaking.** A
jury seat is those three acts in that order. Each frame shows one of them. A pedia post
carries one image, so the set splits naturally into a short sequence.

What the frames do **not** carry is a single new fact about the show. There is no episode
title, no founder name, no company, no sector and no figure in any of them. Everything
that makes a post worth reading still comes from the public record that is already in
`BU_CONTEXT.md`, and every line of it is already sourced.

So the work is not "find something to say about the photo". The work is: take the record
we already have, and hang it on the part of the job the photo shows.

## 3. The one thing that will bite you

**The only frame that shows the show's name also shows an unnamed third party.**

IMG_2618 is the strongest image of the three. It is also the only one with a consent
question, because there is a second juror in it whose face is not identified by any frame,
sidecar or file metadata. He has a name, and we do not have it.

I tried to crop around the problem and it cannot be done both ways at once: crop to Puneet
and you lose the backdrop with the show's name; crop to keep the name and you keep the
other juror. That is a real constraint, not a preference.

So there are two versions of that frame, and they are for different posts:

- `IMG_2618.jpg` — the full frame. **Held** until the other juror's consent or the show's
  release is confirmed. It is the better image and the better post; it just cannot ship
  on our word alone.
- `IMG_2618_crop.jpg` — Puneet alone, 4:5, no third party. Publishable today.

Everything else in the dump is his own face, so it carries no third-party consent issue.

## 6. The strategic point the seniors will actually want

The three frames give the profile something it does not currently have, and it is not
"another event post".

**He was on the selection side of a table that took 5,000–6,000 applications down to
thirty, then six.** For a founder audience, being one of the people who decides is a
stronger authority position than any award on the profile. An award says a jury liked him.
A jury seat says he is the jury.

That position is worth a theme, not a post: **the gatekeeper's view** — what a founder
sounds like when they are ready, what gets cut in the first minute, why the note you write
matters more than the question you ask out loud. Every one of those is a first-person
Family N post, and all of them can be written from the frames we now have, without one
invented fact.

That is the answer to "more relevancy". Not more posts about the show. More posts from the
seat.

## 9. Two things I found in the engine while doing this

Both are small, and both matter more than they look.

**G7 checks that a source id exists, not that it supports the sentence.** SP-010 tags four
opinion paragraphs with `[R-60]`, and `R-60` is a fact about shooting dates. It passes,
and it should not feel like it does. The fix is a warn-only gate that compares each
paragraph with the claim text of its receipt and flags a paragraph that shares no content
word with it. Worth adding before the batch grows.

**G10 runs at 5-grams, and that lets 4-gram echoes through.** Two of my four drafts had
one — "i spent most of" in SP-012 was a genuine echo of his voice, and the gate passed it.
I caught both by hand and both are fixed. Recommend dropping `ngram_overlap` to 4 for
family N, where his voice is closest and an echo is most visible.
