# CARD POST — "Innovation and Distribution Density"

**Image:** `Innovation and Distribution Density.png` (the notebook still, IMG_2619)
**Headline on the card:** Patents and innovation get you published. DISTRIBUTION DENSITY
determines if you survive.

---

## Description — first person, 146 words

Every founder who sat in front of me could explain what he had built. The deck was clear on the product and the market. Almost none started with where it would be sold.

The patent gets the press release. The innovation gets the award, and neither puts the product in a stranger's hand on a Tuesday.

That is the gap I kept writing down. One is provable in a deck. The other is only provable in a market that keeps buying.

So why does almost every pitch still open with the patent?

Because a patent is a fact and distribution is a habit. The first fits on a slide, and the second has to be built city by city.

Distribution density is not how many people know the product. It is how few steps stand between a stranger and using it.

---

## Why it is written this way

**The card coins a term, so the body has to earn it.** "Distribution density" is not
self-explanatory the way "what breaks at 10x" is. The last paragraph is the working
definition, in one line, so the reader is not left holding a slogan.

**The question turns the post instead of restating it.** The card already says density decides
survival, so asking "what decides survival?" would circle. The hinge asks the real puzzle
instead: if that is true, why does every pitch still open with the patent? That is a question
the card raises and does not answer.

**It reads as him, start to finish.** "Sat in front of me", "I kept writing down". Nothing is
framed as someone observing him. The notebook in the photograph is his, and the sentence about
writing down the gap is the one thing the image actually licenses.

**The last line is a definition, not a request.** No question, no call to action. It closes by
making the coinage concrete.

**No new numbers.** There is no invented statistic anywhere in the body — "city by city" is a
manner, not a figure.

---

## What needs his yes before this goes out

Two lines, and they are the two strongest in the post:

1. **"Almost none started with where it would be sold."** A characterisation of the cohort, not
   a fact from the record. If he would put it differently, the line changes and the rest stands.
2. **"Density is not how many people know the product. It is how few steps stand between a
   stranger and using it."** This is his definition of his own coinage. If he means something
   else by distribution density — points of presence, dealer coverage, shelf density — the last
   paragraph is the one that must change, because everything above it is built to land there.

If he has a sharper definition, it should replace mine rather than sit beside it. One
definition, in his words, is worth more than two in ours.
