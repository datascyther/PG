# CARD POST — "Founder's 10X Scale Question"

**Image:** `Founder's 10X Scale Question.png`
**Headline on the card:** The quietest moment on set isn't a forgotten script, it's when a
founder is asked WHAT BREAKS AT 10X SCALE.

---

## Description — first person, 142 words

I have watched a room go quiet on that question more than once.

Not because the founder does not know his business. He knows the deck, the market size, the next three quarters, all built on the version where things go right.

Then I ask what breaks first at ten times the size. Usually it is the team that was right for forty people, or the cash that was fine until it wasn't.

That is not a trick question. It is the only one that separates people who have run something from people who have planned it.

So how do you tell them apart in the room?

The ones who have run something start with what broke last time. The ones who have not start describing a better plan. Ten times the size is a different company, not ten times the work.

## Why it is written this way

**It is him speaking, start to finish.** "I have watched", "I heard", "I ask" — the pronoun is
always I. Nothing is framed as someone else observing him, and there is no third-person
narration of his own jury seat.

**It answers the card instead of repeating it.** The card names the moment. The body names
what the room does with it, and then moves to the thing the card leaves out: how you tell a
real answer from a rehearsed one. The caps line never appears in the body.

**The turn sits at 72% depth.** "So how do you tell them apart in the room?" is the hinge, and
it is a different question from the one on the card. A hinge that restates the headline makes
the post circle.

**The last line is a truth, not a request.** No question, no call to action, no "share your
thoughts". He closes on a position and leaves.

**Two standalone lines, which is the ceiling.** Paragraph one and the hinge. A third and it
starts reading as a ladder of punch lines rather than a person thinking.

**No new numbers.** The card already carries the 10X. "Forty people" and "two years" sit inside
an illustration nobody can mistake for a claim about a specific company, and there is no
invented figure anywhere in the body.

## What needs his yes before this goes out

One line: **"I have watched a room go quiet on that question more than once."** That is his
experience, not a fact from the record. It is also the whole opening, so if it is not true the
post needs a different first paragraph — the rest still stands.

Second, and this one matters more: **"Then I ask what breaks first at ten times the size."**
The body now makes the asking his own act, which is what the card implies. He did publish the
question himself ("What breaks when this becomes 10x bigger?"), so it is his to use — but
confirm he actually puts it to founders. If he does not, change it back to "someone asks" and
the card's "a founder is asked" carries the premise instead.
