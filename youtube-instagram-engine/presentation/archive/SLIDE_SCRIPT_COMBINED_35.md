# SPEAKER SCRIPT — YouTube & Instagram Content Strategy for Puneet Gupta

For `presentation/DECK.html` (35 slides). Open it in a browser, press `F` for fullscreen, use the
arrow keys. Each slide has three parts: what is **on screen**, what to **say**, and what to do
**if asked**. Do not read the slides out; talk over them.

**Running time: about 25 minutes**, plus his questions.

**The one message to land:** defence gives him an edge no other founder has; entrepreneurship,
investing, founder life and AI make the channels his and widen the audience. About 40% defence,
60% everything else.

## 1 — Cover
**On screen:** Personal branding for Puneet Gupta. Five domains in one plan.
**Say:** "This is the full content strategy for your YouTube and Instagram. It covers five parts
of your work: defence, entrepreneurship, investing, founder life and AI. Today is about direction.
Execution comes after you sign off."

## 2 — Vision
**On screen:** The most nuanced voice for India's defence and AI future, and the founder and
investor who has built inside it.
**Say:** "One sentence for both channels. The first half is the edge, the second half is the
credibility. Plenty of people talk about defence and AI. Very few have built and invested in it."
**If asked "why both halves?":** a pure defence channel caps the audience; a pure founder channel
has no edge. Together they make a position nobody else holds.

## 3 — Brand Persona
**On screen:** Credible · Veteran · Approachable · Strategic.
**Say:** "Four words every piece is checked against. Credible: every claim comes from your own
record. Veteran: you have been inside the sector. Approachable: complex topics in plain language.
Strategic: we explain why things happen, not just what happened."

## 4 — Anti Positioning
**On screen:** Not preachy · not a pro-politics narrative · not a typical motivator.
**Say:** "Three things the channels will never sound like. This is the filter for every script,
title and thumbnail. If a draft starts preaching or picking a political side, it goes back."

## 5 — Five Domains
**On screen:** Defence, Entrepreneurship, Investor, Founder, AI, each with your numbers. The
balance: about 40/60.
**Say:** "Every domain is backed by something you have actually done: the 2008–2016 defence
technology years, a company built from an IIT Delhi lab to ₹100 crore ARR, the Series A and the
Born Unicorn jury, about 1,000 hires and stepping down as CEO, and your doctorate in generative
AI. Defence is about 40% of output. The other 60% is what makes this a personal brand rather than a
defence page."
**If asked about the AI venture:** themes only. No product, partner or investor is ever named.

## 6 — YouTube divider
**Say:** "YouTube first. It is where depth and long-term authority are built."

## 7 — YouTube Content Index
**On screen:** 12 pillars in four tracks: Flagship, Defence & Strategy, Future & AI, Building &
Capital.
**Say:** "Twelve pillars, grouped into four tracks. The podcast is the flagship. Six pillars carry
defence and strategy, two carry AI and the future, and three are about building companies and
capital. Each pillar also lists the domains it draws on, so even the defence pillars have a
founder or investor angle."

## 8 — Podcast with Puneet
**On screen:** 60–75 minute conversations: idea, why, the wider guest list, references.
**Say:** "The flagship. Long conversations with people from India's security world, the human
stories and the decisions. Few Indian shows cover this properly, and none have a host with your
link to the sector. The same chair also hosts founders, investors and AI builders, which nobody
else combines."
**If asked about frequency:** two a month (slide 33).

## 9 — Probable Guests
**On screen:** Military leaders, defence analysts, special forces and veterans, intelligence
specialists, founders and investors, AI and tech builders.
**Say:** "Six guest groups. The founder, investor and AI names all come from your own network.
Nobody gets approached until you agree the list."
**Ask him:** "Which three would you want first?"

## 10 — Podcast Theme
**On screen:** About the Person → Professional Life → Insights → Future.
**Say:** "Every episode follows the same arc. The person first for emotional connection, then their
career as a story, then insights on national security, building, capital and AI, then the future.
Viewers know what to expect and still get surprised."

## 11 — On Ground Stories
**Say:** "Visits to bases, academies, labs and shipyards: access most audiences never get. Beyond
defence, we also go inside the incubators where defence-tech startups begin."

## 12 — War Breakdown
**Say:** "Historic operations broken down as decisions: what failed and how we would solve it today.
A history channel narrates; you analyse the decisions. The founder angle is what a builder can
learn from those decisions."

## 13 — Defence Gaps
**Say:** "Most defence content celebrates. This pillar discusses weaknesses: budgets, imports, the
manufacturing challenge. It positions you as a strategic critic. The investor angle is why private
capital still avoids defence-tech."

## 14 — The Strategic Affairs
**Say:** "Policy and geopolitics, and what they mean for India's security, plus topical content.
The entrepreneur angle is what a new export rule means for an Indian startup."

## 15 — Versus Series
**Say:** "India against other countries: where we lead, where we lag, what we can learn. No
chest-thumping. Your angle is how Israel turns defence research into startups, which you saw first
hand on your 2009 visit."

## 16 — Weapon of the Week
**Say:** "One system a week in eight to ten minutes: how it works, does India have it, how it
compares. The weekly habit that brings people back. The extra beat is the Indian company that
builds it."

## 17 — Vision 2035
**Say:** "What India must build in the next decade: skills, technologies, productivity. It casts you
as a visionary rather than a commentator, and it is where the AI stack India needs comes in."

## 18 — AI Decoded
**Say:** "AI for decision-makers: what it means for national security, for business, and for India
owning its own technology. You study it formally and you build in it, so you are a practitioner,
not a commentator."
**If asked:** themes only. Nothing about the current venture's products.

## 19 — Defence Startup Decoded
**Say:** "This is where defence and entrepreneurship meet. How a founder wins a first defence
contract, what iDEX changed, why startups stall between trial and order. You built in this sector
from 2008 to 2016. Very few people can explain it from the inside, and it turns the defence audience
into a business audience."
**Ask him:** "What are you comfortable saying on camera about those years?"

## 20 — The Investor's Table
**Say:** "Pitch content is huge in India, but it is all shot from the founder's chair. You have
raised money and you have judged on a jury: 5,000 to 6,000 applications down to 6. This shows how
the decision is actually made."

## 21 — Founder's Playbook
**Say:** "One real decision per episode: 1,000 hires, stepping down as CEO while the company was
growing, the board advice you still carry. Founders who have been through an acquisition rarely
talk about the decisions with the numbers attached."

## 22 — Instagram divider
**Say:** "Instagram does a different job: faster, more personal, and it brings people to YouTube."

## 23 — Instagram Vision
**On screen:** An insider voice on geopolitics, defence and AI, and on how India's founders and
investors build that future.
**Say:** "Same position, shorter format, more personal."

## 24 — Instagram Content Index
**On screen:** 8 pillars in four tracks: Founder's Journey, Defence & Strategy, Building & Capital,
Future & AI.
**Say:** "Eight pillars. Three are defence and strategy, two are your own journey, two are building
and capital, and one is AI."

## 25 — Journey Decoded
**Say:** "Your story, candidly: what shaped you, why you stepped down from Clensta, the vision
behind your stealth AI venture. This is what makes the account feel human."

## 26 — Strategic Frameworks
**Say:** "How commanders think, applied to business and careers, plus your own frameworks, like the
one you used to decide to step down. It moves the content from defence stories to performance and
thinking, which anyone ambitious can use."

## 27 — First-principles Geopolitics
**Say:** "World events explained from first principles, and why a blockade reaches the common man's
wallet. Topical and AI-assisted. The founder angle is what it does to an Indian startup's costs."

## 28 — Tech & Defence
**Say:** "Technology, data and compute as the new borders: foreign AI models, chip monopolies,
undersea cables. Your experience in the sector is what makes this credible."

## 29 — Heroes' Journey
**Say:** "Instagram audiences connect with people more than with platforms, so these are stories of
soldiers, officers and defence innovators, including innovators who became founders."

## 30 — Startup Breakdown
**Say:** "Short breakdowns of how Indian startups win or lose: one decision, one number. These are
among the most-saved formats on Instagram, and you have built, raised and exited."

## 31 — The Investor Lens
**Say:** "Reels from the jury's side: the first question you ask a founder, the red flags you see.
Founders rarely get the investor's view."
**Ask him:** "What is the first question you ask every founder?" Write his answer down word for
word; it is the first reel.

## 32 — AI in 60 Seconds
**Say:** "One AI idea a week in plain language, for founders and business leaders. It reaches people
who would never follow a defence page."

## 33 — Monthly Cadence & Mix
**On screen:** 6 long-form videos, 2 podcasts, 16 reels and 4 carousels a month, split by track,
plus 10–12 Shorts and 10–12 podcast clips.
**Say:** "This is the monthly output and how it splits. Eleven of 28 core pieces are defence, about
40%. The rest build the founder, investor and AI voice. Shorts and clips are cut from the long-form
videos and podcasts, so they need no extra shooting."
**If asked "why not more defence?":** defence is the edge, but a channel that is only defence
reaches only defence viewers. This split keeps the edge and grows the audience.

## 34 — Process
**On screen:** Pre-production → Production → Post-production → After publishing; every fact
checked, his yes before anything goes live.
**Say:** "Four stages from strategy to monthly reports. Two rules sit on top: every fact is checked
against your public record, and nothing publishes without your yes."

## 35 — Close
**On screen:** Let's build it together.
**Say:** "That's the full direction. If you're happy with the vision and the five domains, we'll
come back with the execution plan: first episodes, first guests and the shoot schedule."
**Then stop talking.**

## Objections to expect

**"Why not only defence? That's the niche."** The niche is the edge, not the whole audience. Defence
stays the biggest single track. The other domains are where his own record is strongest.

**"Will this look like I'm promoting my company?"** No. The AI venture is themes only, and Clensta
appears only as a story.

**"How much of my time does this take?"** Shoot days, podcast recordings and one approval meeting.
The detail comes with the execution plan.

**"What if I don't like a piece?"** One word kills it. Nothing publishes without his yes.
