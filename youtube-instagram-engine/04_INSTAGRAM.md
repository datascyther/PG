# 04 — INSTAGRAM

The deck gives Instagram one slide: a dedicated page, podcast clips, 10–12 a month. That is
volume without a product. This file builds the product.

Instagram has one job on this brand, and it is not reach:

> **Instagram is where a person decides he is worth an hour of their time.**

That is why the grid has to be legible in nine posts, and why a clip with no number in it is
worse than no post at all.

---

## 1. Three formats, no more

| Format | Length | Job | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reel** | 20–45 s | the number, the admission, or the objection — one of the three, never all three | a cut from a long-form episode, or shot natively |
| **Carousel** | 6–8 slides | one argument carried by one receipt per slide | a Teardown or a Gap episode, re-cut |
| **Card** | 1 image | one line, burned on | the existing `HEADLINE_FORMAT.md` spec — already built and already gated |

Nothing else. No photo dumps, no "grateful for this", no reposted screenshots of a LinkedIn
post — a screenshot of text is the single clearest signal that a feed is automated.

The card format already exists and does not need reinventing: the three in-house cards
(`CARD_10X_SCALE_QUESTION.md`, `CARD_DISTRIBUTION_DENSITY.md`,
`CARD_DEFENDING_FOUNDERS.md`) are Instagram-native, and their rule — exactly one emphasis
device per card, always on the thing you would screenshot — is the right rule here too.

---

## 2. The first nine posts (the grid on day one)

Nine is not arbitrary: it is what a visitor sees before deciding to follow. The order below
is the order of reading, left to right, top to bottom.

| # | Format | The post | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Card | "20 ml replaced an entire bath. The maths was the hard part." | R-22, R-42 |
| 2 | Reel | the warehouse at 06:40 on a festival Sunday, one number | M-28, R-06 |
| 3 | Carousel | six slides: how a lab patent becomes a factory line, and where it usually dies | R-08, R-43 |
| 4 | Card | "Six units. 170,000 sq ft. The first thing I looked at was governance." | R-11, M-38 |
| 5 | Reel | the admission — what he mistook for empathy | M-41, R-41 |
| 6 | Carousel | five slides: 5,000 applications, 30, 6 — what a jury actually does | R-75…R-88 |
| 7 | Card | "India has the patents. The line is the hard part." | R-08 |
| 8 | Reel | the objection — the question a founder did not expect | from episode 1 |
| 9 | Carousel | 2035: what has to exist, and who pays for it | R-45 |

Rules that make the grid cohere:

- **Rows tell a story.** Row 1 is *what he makes*, row 2 is *what it costs*, row 3 is *where it
  breaks*. Never post two cards in a row.
- **One face per row.** Three faces stacked reads as a personal-brand account; one face and
  two objects reads as a channel.
- **The same typeface on every card.** Locked once, in `06_PRODUCTION.md` §5.

## 3. Captions

The caption is not a summary. It is the second half of the card.

```
[Line 1 — the number or the admission. ≤ 12 words. Ends with a full stop.]

[2–3 short paragraphs. One receipt per paragraph, no adjectives doing a number's work.
The last paragraph is the turn — what it means, said plainly.]

[One neutral line: where the full episode is.]

#tag1 #tag2 #tag3
```

Rules:

- **The first line carries a number or an admission.** Never "So this happened…".
- **3–5 lowercase hashtags**, no brand tags borrowed from anyone. The same rule as the
  LinkedIn engine's G11.
- **No link in the caption body.** Instagram does not make it clickable; it only makes the
  caption look like an ad. "Full episode in bio" is the whole CTA.
- **No emoji on a business post.** The dossier allows emoji on family, festival and event
  posts only; none of these are.
- **≤ 2,200 characters**, and the useful part must be readable before "more".

## 4. Stories and highlights

Stories are the cheapest surface and the one most likely to be skipped. Four highlights only,
each with a job:

| Highlight | What lives there |
|---|---|
| **On the Floor** | every field clip, in order, unedited |
| **Teardown** | the object clips and the answer to "does India make it" |
| **The Gap** | the admissions, collected — the most-shared material on the account |
| **Ask** | questions from the audience with his answers |

Story cadence: three frames on a shoot day, one frame on any other day. No countdowns, no
polls about his own content.

---

## 5. Cadence

| Day | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | 1 card | pairs with the LinkedIn Monday slot; the same idea, different surface |
| Wednesday | 1 reel | the mid-week number |
| Friday | 1 carousel | the weekend read, saves over likes |
| Shoot days | 3 story frames | proof of work, not promotion |

That is three feed posts a week, which is the same rhythm as LinkedIn and the same rhythm as
YouTube. One calendar, three surfaces — see `05_CADENCE.md`.

**What "10–12 clips a month" actually means:** it is not 10–12 posts. It is 10–12 *assets*,
of which four or five are good enough to publish. The deck's number is an output target for a
shoot, not a posting schedule.

## 6. One handle or two — the deck's open question

The deck asks for "a dedicated Instagram page for podcast shorts". Recommendation: **do not
split, yet.**

| | One handle (recommended) | Two handles |
|---|---|---|
| Followers | one audience compounding | two audiences at half size and half speed |
| Discovery | reels reach strangers anyway; a second page adds nothing | the second page starts at zero, in the same niche |
| Proof | nine posts can carry a person | a clips-only page has no host, so it reads as a media page |
| Cost | one calendar, one approval | two calendars, two approvals, twice the chase |
| When it wins | — | only past ~50k followers, when clips genuinely drown out the rest |

The deck's instinct is right about the *format* — clips are the growth engine — and wrong
about the *container*. Put the clips on his handle. The rule that follows: on the day clips
outnumber other posts 4:1 and the account has 50k followers, revisit this with data, not
with preference.

---

## 7. What Instagram is measured on

Not likes. In order:

1. **Saves per reach** — the only signal that someone intends to use the thing.
2. **Shares per reach** — the only signal that travels without an ad.
3. **Profile visits per post** — the actual job described at the top of this file.
4. **Follows from profile visits** — whether the nine-post grid works.

Likes are a by-product and reach is a vanity metric: his own export shows the trade-off
directly (correlation −0.31 between impressions and engagement rate; the 74,295-impression
post converted at 0.21%).
