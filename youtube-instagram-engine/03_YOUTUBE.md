## 2. Episode anatomy

### 2a. Teardown — the workhorse (8–10 min, 1,200–1,500 words of script)

| Beat | Time | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00–0:15 | one claim and one object | ≤ 12 words, no question, no "hey guys" |
| The object | 0:15–1:30 | show it. hands on it. | the thing must be on screen before the first edit |
| How it works | 1:30–4:00 | three moving parts, no more | each part gets one number |
| What it costs | 4:00–6:00 | the number nobody says out loud | must trace to a receipt |
| Does India make it | 6:00–7:30 | yes / partly / no, and the honest reason | the deck's best question; keep it exactly |
| Where it is weak | 7:30–9:00 | the admission | this is the differentiator; never skip it |
| Close | 9:00–9:30 | a fact, not a request | never end on "like and subscribe" |

### 2b. The Long Conversation (60–75 min)

The deck's seven beats are good enough to keep as-is. Converted to a running order:

1. **The person** (0–8) — where they grew up working, not where they studied
2. **The emotional connection** (8–18) — the thing they are still angry or proud about
3. **Professional life** (18–35) — the actual job, in operational detail
4. **Storytelling and suspense** (35–48) — the decision they have never told publicly
5. **Insight** (48–60) — the one thing they would tell a room of 20-year-olds
6. *(beat 6 of the deck is Chapter D — replaced by)* **The gap they see** (60–68) — what their own industry is bad at
7. **Future, tech, hope** (68–75) — what they are building next and what worries them

At 150 words per minute, 60–75 minutes is **9,000–11,000 words of speech**. That is not a
script; it is a running order plus six questions per beat. Write the questions, not the
answers — a scripted podcast sounds scripted.

### 2c. On the Floor (3–8 min)

The only format that cannot be made in a room. Field audio, one continuous walk, no cutaways
to a desk. One person who works there explains one thing. He asks four questions and then
stops talking. The number arrives from the person who owns it, not from him.

Length is whatever the floor gives you: usually the shortest cut first, then a longer one when
the answers carry it. A three-minute field piece with one real number beats a nine-minute one
with four rehearsed answers.

## 3. Titles

Rules, in order of frequency of violation:

- **≤ 60 characters**, so nothing truncates in search.
- **Carry the number.** "Six units, 170,000 sq ft, and a governance problem nobody priced."
- **No question marks.** A question title asks the viewer to do the work.
- **No superlatives doing a number's work** — "insane", "mind-blowing", "you won't believe".
- **Name the object.** A title with no noun is a title about a mood.

Worked examples, all sourced:

| Episode | Title | Chars |
|---|---|---|
| Teardown | 20 ml replaced an entire bath. Here is the maths | 49 |
| On the Floor | Four lakh units, one Sunday, the whole team | 44 |
| Post-Mortem | A ₹100 crore brand and the day the packaging changed | 56 |
| Versus | India and Vietnam, same order, different cost per unit | 57 |
| The Gap | India has the patents. It does not have the line | 48 |
| 2035 | What has to exist in India by 2035, and who pays | 53 |

## 4. Thumbnails

Five words maximum, one object, one number, his face only when he is in the frame of the
story. No arrows, no red circles, no shocked expression.

| Episode type | Thumbnail |
|---|---|
| Teardown | the object, close, on a plain surface + "20 ML" |
| On the Floor | him mid-floor, in the frame with a worker, nobody posed + "4 LAKH / ONE SHIFT" |
| Post-Mortem | the artefact that marks the failure — the old packaging, the returned pallet + "₹100 CR" |
| The Gap | a diagram with one part missing + "THE MISSING LINE" |
| 2035 | one plant silhouette, nothing else + "2035" |

## 5. Shorts — the three-cut rule

One long episode produces at most three Shorts, and each one is a different *kind*:

| Cut | What it is | Length |
|---|---|---|
| the number | the single most surprising figure, cold open, no context | 20–35 s |
| the admission | the sentence where he says what is weak | 30–45 s |
| the objection | the question he asks that the guest did not expect | 15–25 s |

Never cut a Short that begins with "so the other day I was talking to…". Never post a Short
that has no number in the first three seconds. Three Shorts per episode is the ceiling; ten
is a content farm.
