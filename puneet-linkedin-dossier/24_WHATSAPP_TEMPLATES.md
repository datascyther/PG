# 24_WHATSAPP_TEMPLATES.md — every message Puneet will receive

> **INTERNAL ONLY — DO NOT SHARE.** The manager's copy-paste library. Not for external

> distribution.

**Created:** 2026-10-21 · **Design rule for all four:** one message, one ask, one reply

word. Never two asks in one message. Never a draft he has to edit.

---

# 1 · THE DAILY CAPTURE (07:45 IST, every day)

> Send me one thing from today — what happened, where, and what it made you think. Voice

> note is fine. "Nothing today" is also fine.

**Frequency: WEEKLY, not daily.** The batch model needs material for the *next* batch, not this
one - we already hold 30 unspent live moments from his own published writing. So the prompt runs
once a week, on Saturday morning, and any answer joins the next batch. If he sends something on a
Tuesday, it is a bonus, not an expectation.

**Why it works:** 26 words, one sentence, answerable in under 60 seconds, and "nothing

today" is a valid answer so the prompt can never feel like a failure. Voice note first

because a 45-second recording costs him less than a typed sentence.

**Never:** follow it up. No "did you see my message", no second nudge, no apology for

silence. If he is travelling or blocked, skip the day — the queue does not accumulate.

---

# 2 · THE WEEKLY MEETING INVITE (Sunday evening or Monday morning)

> 20 minutes, same slot every week. Three drafts, three one-word answers. Nothing to

> read in advance, nothing to edit, nothing to approve in writing.

**Why it works:** names the time cost (20 minutes) and the shape (three drafts, three

words) before he commits. "Nothing to read in advance" removes the two things people

dread most about a content review.

**Never:** send this more than once a week, and never on a day he has said he is away.

---

# 3 · THE APPROVAL MESSAGE — the batch, one reply

**Send this once, right after the meeting.** It is the single most important message in
the system: it converts a decision he already made in the room into a publishable reply.

**It carries the whole batch — 7 posts for the next fortnight, numbered — plus the 3
questions that are still open.** He replies with a list of numbers and nothing else.

```
Seven posts, the next two weeks. All of them are built on something you already said or
wrote, so reply with just the numbers you want to go out.

--- 1 ---
Every fast company has a second system nobody designed.

I have hired close to a thousand people, and the same three things slowed us down every time. Escalations, edge cases, and one person sitting inside the system waiting to approve.

That last one is the expensive one, because from the outside it looks like diligence. An escalation reaches a founder and gets treated as a service problem. It is not a service problem. It is a routing problem.

So where does your team actually slow down? On the work itself, or on the person who has to say yes first?

The decision was never missing. What was missing was the path to it. Scale does not remove that person. It only moves them somewhere less visible.

--- 2 ---
I got 96% in the paper on pre-trained models, and 96% in the case study. The marks were not the strange part.

The strange part was this. I had spent nine years running companies, and then somebody sat me down and graded what I had written.

Going back to a classroom is its own kind of strange. So here we are. Now the numbers come from evaluation sheets, not from CAC, LTV and ARR.

So who taught you the most, and when did you last sit in a room where you were the one being taught?

The best years of my learning so far have been the ones where I had no standing at all.

--- 3 ---
The interesting part was not the jackets.

Sergi, Maria, Olga and Anna were wearing Indian jackets I had carried for them, holding Indian sweets, asking questions about India. You arrive with fabric and mithai. You leave with curiosity about how a country thinks, builds and scales.

So if someone asked you how your country actually builds things, what would you say in the first thirty seconds?

Cloth still carries the hands that made it, and food still carries the season it came from. What is changing is that our systems are beginning to carry ambition as well.

--- 4 ---
The festive season does not show up in a forecast. It shows up as a Sunday morning in a warehouse.

In 2023, before the season, we were getting four lakh products ready. I walked in on Sunday mornings, because that was when the plan met the reality. Packers working, orders changing. Every process that was supposed to be simple had a person attached to it.

The best thing I learned about scaling came from standing on that floor, not from a dashboard. A dashboard tells you what happened. A floor tells you what is about to happen.

What would change if you spent one Sunday there instead of in a review meeting?

Everyone looks at a festive season from above, where it is a number going up. From the floor it is a hundred problems at once, and a hundred people who know what to do.

--- 5 ---
In the beginning, the founder is the answer to every unfinished question.

At Clensta I answered them until the company was 500 people. I approved the spend, settled the arguments, decided what came next, and for a while that is exactly why we moved faster than anyone else.

Then the same instinct becomes the ceiling. Every decision still comes back to me, and the company moves at the speed of one person's calendar.

Which of them still need me, and which only feel like they do?

Delegating harder rarely fixes this. The real job is telling the two apart, and then letting the rest run without me.

--- 6 ---
Every founder runs more than one list, and the mistake is treating them as one list.

The method I use is simple. A is what cannot move without me. B is what matters but can wait a month. C is the long game that nobody will thank me for this quarter.

The hard part is not the sorting. It is refusing to pull a C task up just because it feels more exciting than an A task.

So which of your three lists keeps stealing time from the one that keeps the lights on?

Most operators are excellent at A and guilty about C. The ones who last are the ones who protect C on purpose.

--- 7 ---
I handed my list of questions to the room. It is not what a host is supposed to do.

I had a fireside with Bhuvneshwar Kumar on stage, and I had written down the questions I wanted to ask. Then I asked the audience for one, and the best one would be asked on stage. [ask: what was the question that won, and what did he answer?]

A host with a list reads his list. A host with a room gets a better question.

If you had one question for a person you admire, what would it be?

The best conversations I have had were the ones where somebody in the room asked the thing I was too close to see.

Reply in any of these ways:
• "1, 3, 6"  = publish those three
• "1 no, 2 yes, 3 not me"  = as much detail as you want, but the short version works
• "none"  = none of them, and that is a fine answer
• "not me" on a specific one = say which, I will drop it

Three things I still need, only for the ones you approve:
1. "500+ people led" - can that be pinned to one company and a year, or do I keep it as an
   aggregate?
2. The four names in post 5 (Sergi, Maria, Olga, Anna) - and Shivali, Jack Dorsey, Amazon, the
   investors, the adviser, and the companies in your current roles - which of those am I free to
   put in your voice? Give me a list, or just say "none of those".
3. Post 6: what was the question that won the stage, and what did he answer? Post 7: what does
   Diwali look like at home for you, and what are you building next?

Nothing goes out without your yes. If I do not hear back, it waits.
```

**Why it works:** he has already seen the batch in the meeting, so this message is a form, not
a reading task. "1, 3, 6" is the fastest possible reply, and one message covers two weeks of
publishing, so his inbox stays quiet between meetings. The three questions are grouped at the
end so they do not interrupt the yes/no flow.

**Never:** chase the reply. Silence = held, not published. A no needs no explanation and no
apology, and it never counts against the next post.

# 4 · THE LIVE CAPTURE DEMO (do this in the meeting, 2 minutes)

> Before we finish — send me one thing that happened today, right now, as a voice note.
> Sixty seconds. Anything. A meeting, a call, something that annoyed you, a place you
> walked through. I want to see how this works before I ask you to do it every day.

**Why it works:** it converts an abstract instruction into a 60-second action he has

just watched himself perform. It also produces real material on the spot, which is the

point: the first capture is a demonstration, not a request.

**If he says he has nothing:** accept it gracefully — "that is a completely normal

answer, and it is the same answer the prompt will accept tomorrow." Then start the

daily prompt anyway. Do not push.

---

# 5 · THE HELD NOTE (only if he replies with a number we cannot publish yet)

> Got it. Draft 3 is held until I hear where you were — no rush, it will wait.

**Why:** silent, one line, no pressure. It closes the loop so he knows the answer was

received, and it makes the hold feel normal rather than like a blocker.
