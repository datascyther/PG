# 22_MEETING_CARD.md — The 20-minute meeting with Puneet

> **INTERNAL ONLY — DO NOT SHARE.** Manager's card for the meeting. Print it, do not send it.
> Not for external distribution.

**Created:** 2026-10-21
**Length:** 20 minutes, same slot every week, six questions, one meeting.
**Success condition:** he leaves having given **six one-line answers** and having installed **one
daily prompt**. Nothing else.

---

## 0. The frame, in the first 60 seconds (say it out loud)

> "Nothing here needs you to write or edit anything. You will never see a draft to edit. I need six
> answers, two of them one-word answers, and I need one small habit: one line a day, in a voice
> note, about something that actually happened to you. That is the whole ask."

Then this, because it is the sentence that makes the rest work:

> "Anything we publish waits for your yes. One word — 'no' — kills it, no explanation, and I will
> never ask why. The only reason I am asking you at all is so that the posts sound like you
> rather than like us."

---

## 1. The daily habit (5 minutes — install it in the room)

Read this aloud and get him to do one, live:

> **"Send me one thing from today — what happened, where, and what it made you think. Voice note is
> fine. 'Nothing today' is also fine."**

Then ask, in this order:

1. "Can you do 45 seconds on this, right now, in a voice note?" → **do one together, live.** This is
   the highest-value five minutes of the meeting: it proves the channel works in the room.
2. "What time of day will you do this without resenting it?" → set the slot (default 07:45 IST).
3. "WhatsApp only, or do you also want a way to send me one thing to react to?" → catch
   notification fatigue early.
4. "Is this template fine, or do you want it simpler?" → optional three slots: *What happened /
   Where / What stuck* — he can ignore the third.

**Cap his time: 60 seconds a day + this meeting.** Say it. Then keep to it.

## 2. The real-world anchors we need (5 minutes)

This is the substance. We already hold **40 dated, real moments** from his public writing and
**~30 unspent one-line mentions** — real material he published but never expanded. We are not
asking him to write posts. We are asking him to **fill in the detail** on the moments he already
owns.

Read this to him:

> "You have already written the opening line of about thirty stories and then stopped. Some of them
> are sitting unused — they are real, they are yours, and they are published. I am not asking you
> to write anything. I am asking you to give me one detail for the ones you want used."

Then read these out and ask for **one line each** — no more:

| His own line (already public) | The one detail we need |
|---|---|
| "Tomorrow, I'll be at Indian Institute of Technology, Delhi…" | What did you actually tell them? One sentence. |
| "Looking forward to some interesting questions, honest conversations…" | What question landed? What did you answer? |
| "Best one gets asked on stage." | What was the question, and what did you say? |
| "Then I took him into a narrower conversation: 'From Performance to Entrepreneurship.'" | What did he say that made you go narrower? |
| "IUVA Labs is now building out of IIT Bombay." | What is the hardest part of that build-out right now? |
| "I joined recently, and what struck me is how seriously the room takes thinking itself." | Which room, and what was the first question you were asked? |
| "I am here for three or four more days… coffee is on me." | Who did you actually meet? |
| "Most smart people make simple things sound complicated…" | What did you bring him that day? |
| "Sat on a staircase high up in Switzerland." | Why did you sit down? |
| "At some point during that journey, I had the opportunity to spend time with…" | Where and when? |
| "Back in 2009, my first international technology visit was to Israel." | What did you see being built that you came home copying? |
| "When Vinod Khosla was in India. I got the room." | Which city, which room, what did he ask you? |
| "Our conversation drifted towards how difficult decisions are made…" | Which decision? Which state? What was the answer? |
| "The escalations. The edge cases. The human 'approvals' sitting quietly inside systems." | What was the worst example you had to fix? |
| "We all started our mornings the same way… the same photocopy machine :)" | What did that job teach you that you still use? |
| "Sunday Morning in the warehouse with the entire team…" | What was going wrong that Sunday? |

**Rule: one detail, one line, no essay.** If he gives three, take the first one and move on.

**Also ask, once:** *"Is there anything from the last two years that you think should be on record
and is not — a number, a milestone, a decision — that we do not already have?"*

## 5. What NOT to do in this meeting

- **Do not read a draft.** Not one. Reading a draft converts the meeting into an approval meeting
  and he will start editing.
- **Do not ask him to review the calendar.** He defends days; the manager runs the calendar.
- **Do not ask "what do you think about this post".** One factual question per draft, maximum.
- **Do not explain the analytics to him.** He does not need the ER table; he needs the prompt to
  work.
- **Do not let it run past 20 minutes.** If it does, the next meeting is shorter.

## 7. The question to have an answer for before the meeting

**"We cannot match his writing style."** The answer, in one line:

> We are not trying to sound like him. We are putting his own real moments back in front of him
> and letting him approve them. The style is in the material. A post built on something that
> actually happened to him, dated and specific, sounds like him — because it is him. A post built
> on an idea sounds like whoever wrote it, no matter how carefully we copy his sentence shapes.

That is also the growth argument, and it is the one that survives being questioned: **the posts
that carry a real anchor are the posts that already work.** 25 of his 50 best-performing posts name
a place or an institution, 25 name a third party, 22 use a number as evidence. His best post of the
year earned 1,155 engagements on 6,010 impressions — 19.22% — and it is about an athlete
under pressure, in a room he was actually in. The largest post of the year, 74,295 impressions,
earned 159 engagements, 0.21%. Depth came from the room, not from the reach.
