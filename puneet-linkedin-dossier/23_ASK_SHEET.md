## 0b. The meeting order — drafts first, the daily line last

**The two asks are not alternatives. They do different jobs, and they run in this order.**

| # | Block | Minutes | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The batch** — 7 drafts for the next fortnight, one word each | 7 | He answers with **yes / no / not me**. A multiple-choice ask is the lowest-friction thing we can put in front of him, and his pattern of answers tells us what he likes without him having to explain it. One meeting covers two weeks of publishing, so his touchpoint is once a fortnight, not three times a week |
| 2 | The one fact each of four drafts needs | 5 | Converts a yes into a publishable post. Three of the seven need nothing at all |
| 3 | The three name / attribution questions | 3 | Only what the public record cannot settle |
| 4 | The five decisions only he can make | 3 | Days, authority, prompt time, Diwali, the two facts |
| 5 | **The live capture demo** | 2 | *"Send me one voice note right now about something that happened today."* This is the real goal of the meeting — it converts an abstract instruction into a 60-second action he has just watched himself perform, and it gives us real material on the spot |

**Why the drafts come first:** a review he can answer in one word feels like respect for his time;
an open request to "give me a real context" does not, and it often returns nothing. But the drafts
are a one-off — they do not scale, and five "not me" answers leave us with no material. The daily
line is what makes the system run without us needing him every week, so the meeting must end by
installing it, and the live demo is the way to install it.

**If he has nothing to send in the live demo:** accept it. *"That is a completely normal answer,
and it is the same answer the prompt will accept tomorrow."* Then start the daily prompt anyway.
Do not push.

**If he says "none of these" to all five:** that is a real answer and it is useful. Ask one
question: *"which of these is closest — the subject, the length, or the ending?"*

## 6. What happens in the 72 hours after a yes

1. Apply his answer to the one open line; re-run the nine gates (`18_GATE_LOG.md`).
2. Staleness sweep 3 hours before publish — strip any relative time, re-check the subject age.
3. Publish in the next Mon / Wed / Fri 08:30 slot. Manual paste, then **screenshot the published
   post** — a post with no screenshot is treated as unpublished (`19_PUBLISH_LOG.csv`).
4. Freeze the 72-hour metrics, then the 7-day metrics.
5. Friday review: one change only for the following week.
