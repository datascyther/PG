## P1 — FULL TEXT — "I was wrong. And I need to say this out loud." ⭐
- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/puneetgbuilds_i-was-wrong-and-i-need-to-say-this-out-loud-activity-7348658710925168640-MpHi
- **Date:** 2025-07-09
- **Reactions:** 112 | **Comments:** 48 (guest-visible)
- **AI title:** From Defence to Entrepreneurship: What I Learned

> I was wrong. And I need to say this out loud. 20 years ago, I was selected to serve in the Indian Defence. And even today, I carry that mindset — move fast, adapt under fire, survive first, solve later. But that mindset doesn’t always work in business. I used to believe in the idea: “Jab nadi aayegi tab pul banayenge.” That we’ll figure it out when the problem comes. But I was wrong. If you don’t plan ahead, you don’t get a second chance — the chaos, the pressure, and the emergencies will drown you. You don’t build the bridge during the flood. You build it before. I also used to believe that if people got to learn and earn, they’d stay. Later, I added “recognition” to that list — a belief I called LSD: Lakshmi (wealth), Saraswati (knowledge), and Durga (recognition). But I was wrong again. More than learning, earning, or even recognition — great companies are built on processes. Not passion. Not charisma. Not just product. If the engine doesn’t work without the founder, it’s not an engine. It’s a hustle. From aerospace at Honeywell, to building optoelectronics for the armed forces, to inventing waterless tech with IIT Delhi, to building a consumer brand from scratch — Every step was powered by resilience, naivety, and a bit of madness. We turned Clensta from an idea to an international brand. We scaled Clensta to a point where it was valued at $36 million, and it has now been acquired. Cumulatively, I’ve built startups worth over $100 million. What I built taught me a lot. What I lost taught me more. I’m deeply thankful to every investor, customer, and team member who believed, built, and stayed through the highs and lows. Alongside that gratitude, there are also hard truths we rarely speak aloud. But here’s what they don’t tell you in the headlines: Not everything goes right. Some decisions hurt people. Some debts remain. Some lessons are expensive. One of my board members once told me: “Puneet, it will never be smooth sailing.” He was right. And what they don’t tell you is — sometimes the storm comes from inside the ship. “The founder must be the last to leave. You take the hit. You hold the line. You leave the light on.” I’m stepping down as CEO. Not in retreat. But in reflection. Back to zero. Back to stealth. I will share more soon — about the road, the rebuild, and what I’ve learned along the way. In the background, I’m deepening my lens on AI and emerging tech — sharpening tools for what’s next. But for now — I’m taking time to reflect and reset.
