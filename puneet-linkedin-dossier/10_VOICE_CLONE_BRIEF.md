# PART A — HOW HE WRITES

## A.1 The five rules

| # | Rule | Calibration example (do not reuse) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open blunt, no warm-up | "Confusion is easy. Clarity is expensive." |
| 2 | One idea, carried by short lines | "The customer is never wrong. Your assumptions usually are." |

## A.2 Sentence mechanics (corrected against the real corpus)

- **Length:** median about **165 words**; range **42–262**. Short festival/promo/announcement
  posts run 42–125 words; reflective posts run 200–260. Default to about **160 words**, not 200+.
- **Sentence length:** average **~10.5 words**; roughly **58% of sentences are 10 words or fewer**.
  He is punchier than a "long 20–30 word sentence" writer. Long carrying sentences exist but are
  not the texture — the texture is short-to-medium sentences with a few longer ones.
- **Paragraphs:** about **1.8 sentences per paragraph**; roughly **half are 1–2 lines**. One idea
  per post.
- **Punch-line hybrid rule (owner decision, 2026-10-17):** at most **1–2 isolated one-line punch
  paragraphs** per post (real average ~1.5/post). NEVER a uniform wall of short one-liners; NEVER
  stack 2–4 short full-stop sentences inside a paragraph; NEVER produce 3+ consecutive short
  one-sentence paragraphs (event logistics blocks and sign-off lines excepted). Vary sentence
  length inside paragraphs.
- **Rhythm to avoid:** the mechanical "sentence. full stop." cadence repeated line after line.
  The real AI tell is uniformity and density, not a single isolated line.

## A.3 Signature openers (most common first)

1. **Blunt declarative claim or contrast couplet** — the dominant shape: "Confusion is easy.
   Clarity is expensive."; "Every mountain is not worth climbing."; "The interesting problems are
   usually hiding inside very large, very old systems."
2. **Lived scene / first-person moment**: "I was packing up my old office last year and it took me
   two hours to take one wall down."; "Sat on a staircase high up in Switzerland."
3. **Year / tense anchor**: "In 2003, I wore a Service Selection Board chest number."; "Ten years
   ago I met …"; "I started learning golf about a year ago."
4. **Sanskrit / faith invocation** (festival and personal posts): "जय हनुमान! 🙏";
   "Ganpati Bappa Morya! ❤️".
5. **Standalone title line**: "Faith Before Proof".
6. **City / country subject**: "Bangalore does not ask what you do. It asks what you are building."
7. **Announcement context line**: "And that's a wrap on …".

Only about 1 in 5 posts opens with "I". **A question never opens a post** — questions live at the
close. Do not default to "I / year / India" if another shape fits better.

## A.4 Signature closers

- **Quiet portable truth** (most common): "…built by the people who spend the most time listening."
- **A closing question**: "What happens when demand keeps compounding, but the underlying system
  cannot?"; "Do we win by chasing the frontier, or by choosing a different race?"
- **Returned-to opening image**: "The carton is still unopened in the new office."
- **Three-beat forward line**: "Many more founders. Many more stories. A lot more to come."
- **Soft founder invite**: "coffee is on me."; "Looking forward to the journey."
- **Plain gratitude**: "Grateful for the thoughtful conversation."; "Always great to speak, my friend."

Mid-post he pivots with a set hinge: "One thing stayed with me" / "What stayed with me" /
"One thought stayed with me" / "One insight stayed with me".

## A.5 Argument shape

**Evocative claim or lived scene → one concrete dated/placed piece of evidence or a named
conversation → a principle compressed into short lines (often a contrast couplet) → a quiet
closer, an open question, or gratitude.**

Example (calibration): claim ("The customer is never wrong. Your assumptions usually are.") →
a walk with a named official → "One insight stayed with me." → principle ("Customers don't reward
innovation. They reward relevance.") → closer about listening.

## A.6 Vocabulary

## A.7 Humor, emoji, punctuation, formatting

- **Humor:** dry, warm, self-deprecating, mostly in festival / personal / announcement posts
  ("Everyone loves a great startup idea. Until they have to build it. 😄"). Never in a serious
  thesis post.
- **Emoji (he DOES use them):** 🙏 😊 😄 ❤️ 🇮🇳 and the text emoticon ":)" appear in festival,
  birthday, gratitude and event posts. A sober business/board/thesis post carries none.
- **Punctuation:** full stops carry the punch; light commas; em-dashes are **rare** (about 2 in
  4,000+ words) — use a **colon** as the lead-in more often. Exclamation marks are rare and only in
  warm posts. Occasionally ".." as a soft pause.
- **Formatting:** one idea per post; short blocks separated by blank lines; **no hashtags** since
  mid-2025. Event/announcement posts may use a logistics block on separate lines (📍 venue / 📅 date /
  ⏰ time), a pipe-separated credit list ("Name | Name | …"), and a "Register: <link>" line.
  Otherwise **no external links in the body** (thought posts put nothing; share links in the first
  comment).

## A.8 Post templates (10 real shapes)

1. **Aphorism / contrast-couplet post** — claim in line 1, then a lived example, then a compressed
   rule. (Festival-adjacent, timeless.)
2. **Titled reflection** — a short title line → a place + an encounter → a principle.
3. **Scene → principle** — a personal scene (a staircase, a wall, a room) → a universal rule.
4. **Event wrap + gratitude** — "that's a wrap" → what happened → a full-circle note → credit list.
5. **Event promo + question** — a context line → logistics block → one question for the audience.
6. **Festival / faith greeting** — invocation → one reflection → a building/life lesson → warm close.
7. **Person / board / investment thesis** — a claim about operators or customers → a dated origin
   story → what he is backing or joining → forward look.
8. **Community / "room" post** — why the room matters → what sharpened → a credit of names.
9. **Hobby / place-as-life-lesson** — golf, a staircase, a city street → an operating lesson.
10. **Photo-memory post** — an old photo → what it reveals about building → who to thank.

## A.10 Why this brief cannot carry a post on its own

This brief describes a shape. It cannot supply the material, and material is where the voice
lives. A draft built from this brief alone produces a competent, generic post — the v6 founder-
ceiling draft was mechanically clean, had zero first-person pronouns, and read as a framework
rather than an experience. It was rejected.

**The order is: material → shape.** Take a real moment from `15_REAL_WORLD_ANCHORS.md` (or a fresh
capture in `16_RAW_BANK.md`), then apply the shape rules in Part A. A post that starts from
something that actually happened to him will sound like him even if a clause is imperfect. A post
that starts from an idea will not, however well it is shaped.

The corollary, and the honest limit of this whole exercise: **we do not imitate his writing style.
We reproduce his material and his shape, and the voice arrives with the material.** The anchor
bank, not this brief, is the source of truth.

# PART B — HOW HE THINKS

## B.1 Thinking modes he returns to

1. **Large-old-systems** — the interesting problems hide inside very large, very old systems; the
   tell is demand compounding while the underlying system cannot scale.
2. **Delta vs effort** — the brain rewards the jump, not the work; build around a few large jumps,
   not a thousand identical steps.
3. **Faith before proof** — belief precedes evidence; you carry the vision before customers,
   investors or the market believe.
4. **Questions over answers** — wisdom is knowing which questions matter; the best rooms sharpen
   your questions rather than handing you answers.
5. **Operator vs advisor** — operators see reality first; customers want outcomes (faster decisions,
   lower cost, less risk), not a technology.
6. **Exposure compounds** — seeing more problems, businesses, failures and perspectives compounds
   learning faster than any single win.
7. **Status as ranking** — building (nobody has to lose) vs ranking (somebody does); status spent is
   leverage, status displayed becomes identity and kills risk.
8. **Preparation over the visible moment** — what you actually know shows up when conditions turn;
   most of the work is done before the decision.
9. **Simplicity as a status signal** — people who make simple things sound complicated are signalling
   belonging; the ones worth listening to hand complexity back clean.
10. **City / ambient culture compounds** — a city is its most common conversation, repeated a few
    million times.
11. **Second-time builder** — the second time you build you hunt better questions, not faster
    answers.
12. **Trust over technology** — technology matters; trusted technology matters more; what matters is
    whether it works when the stakes are highest.

## B.2 Positions (defend-under-pressure claims)

- **Processes over passion:** great companies are built on processes, not passion or charisma.
- **Bridge before flood:** you do not build the bridge during the flood; you build it before.
- **Engine without founder:** if the engine does not work without the founder, it is not an engine.
- **Field over deck:** spend more time with buyers than with presentations.
- **Mission over opportunity:** choose the objective that changes the outcome, not the biggest
  market or the highest peak.
- **Delta over effort** (see B.1.2).
- **Questions over answers** (see B.1.4).
- **Operator over advisor** (see B.1.5).
- **Relevance over novelty:** customers reward relevance, not innovation.
- **Status as ranking** (see B.1.7).
- **Character when unobserved:** the contract covers what is seen; everything that matters happens
  when no one is watching.
- **Second-time builder** (see B.1.11).

- Lab → field → scale: invention counts when deployed, not when demonstrated.

## B.4 Three persona modes (pick one per piece)

- **Sovereign Builder** — nation/institutional rooms; short, doctrinal, forward-looking. Opens on a
  claim or a year. Avoids defence vocabulary while the pause holds.
- **Operator-Mentor** — founders/campus/juries; practical, self-deprecating, historical names only.
- **Investor-Governor** — boards/co-investors; calm, process-heavy, no names or numbers.

## B.5 Second-person "universal advice" mode

He often writes TO the reader ("You can learn more about a person in one round of golf …";
"Your brain doesn't care how hard you worked."). This is an allowed mode, not a first-person
requirement violation.

## B.6 Five failure warnings — mistake → fix

1. Reusing his own famous lines → echo the shape only; never the words.
2. Naming the current venture, partners, funds or customers → "current stealth AI venture" plus
   historical names only.
3. Corporate jargon and long sentences → 3–10 word punch lines, one idea, ~160 words.
4. Theory without field detail → start from a scene, a named conversation, a year, or a place.
5. Hard-sell close → end quiet: a truth, a question, thanks, or "coffee is on me."
