# 17_DRAFTS — Template and derivation rules

> **INTERNAL ONLY — DO NOT SHARE.** Drafts contain the owner's material before it is published.
> Not for external distribution.

**Created:** 2026-10-21
**Purpose:** the one place a post is written, so that every line can be traced to something the
owner actually said, did, or can be checked against a public record.

---

## 1. The derivation tag system (the anti-ghostwriter mechanism)

Every sentence in every draft carries a marginal source tag. **A line with no tag is deleted.**

| Tag | Meaning | Publishable? |
|---|---|---|
| `[his: …]` | Traceable to the owner's own words or his own material | Yes |
| `[public: …]` | Traceable to his public record — a year, an institution, an award, a historical venture, a press report | Yes, and must carry a year or place anchor |
| `[derived: …]` | A restatement or compression of his words | Yes **only if logically equivalent to what he said, never stronger** |
| `[ask: …]` | A factual question to him | No — not publishable until answered |

**Two deletions that are automatic:**
- A `[derived]` line whose justification is "that sounds like him" → deleted.
- A line that introduces a claim he has not made, however mild → deleted, or converted to `[ask]`.

**The standard to hold:** we may compress his insight; we may never add a claim. Invented
dialogue, invented people, invented numbers, invented dates, invented outcomes and invented
feelings are all forbidden, even when they would read well.

---

## 2. The transformation ladder (five steps, in order)

**Step 1 — Extract the anchor.** From his material pull the object, the place, the number, the
person. If the anchor test fails, stop and return the item to the raw bank. The anchor becomes the
post's spine.

**Step 2 — Open with a restatement, never an escalation.** One blunt declarative claim, ≤14 words,
that says no more than he said. His corpus opens this way in the majority of cases. A question
never opens a post.

**Step 3 — Add the field detail.** One paragraph of scene. Every detail comes from his material
or from already-public dated facts about him.

**Step 4 — Compress the principle.** One contrast couplet or a two-line rule, logically equivalent
to what he said. This is the only place stylistic licence is allowed: the couplet may be sharper
in form, never stronger in claim.

**Step 5 — Close quietly.** A returning image, a portable truth, a hinge question, or plain
thanks. Never a CTA block. A question sits at 55–90% depth, where the argument turns.

**Optional Step 6 — one factual question to him.** Factual, answerable in under 15 seconds,
12-hour deadline. Only if a line cannot be tagged without it.

## 3. Draft file naming and header

`17_DRAFTS/<date>-<slug>-v<N>.md`, with this header:

```
slug:            <short-slug>
status:          DRAFT | GATES PASSED | AWAITING APPROVAL | APPROVED | PUBLISHED | KILLED
pillar:          FC | WS | EV | FF | PS | IE
moment_id:       M-xx / L-xx / N-xx  (from 15_REAL_WORLD_ANCHORS.md)
receipt_id:      R-xx                (from 15_REAL_WORLD_ANCHORS.md)
material_id:     RB-xxx              (from 16_RAW_BANK.md)
subject_date:    YYYY-MM-DD          (when the thing actually happened)
publish_slot:    YYYY-MM-DD 08:30 IST
question:        none | <the one question asked>
tags:            [his] [public] [derived] [ask]
word_count:      n
```

## 4. Draft body format

```
> In the beginning, the founder is the answer to every unfinished question.
>
> At Clensta I answered them until the company was 500 people. I approved the spend,
> settled the disagreement, decided what came next, and for a while that is exactly why
> we moved faster than anyone else.
>
> Then the same instinct becomes the ceiling. Every decision still routes back to me,
> and the company starts moving at the speed of one person's calendar.
>
> Which of them still need me, and which only feel like they do?
>
> Delegating harder rarely fixes this. The real job is telling the two apart, then
> letting the rest run without me.

[his: "the founder is the answer to every unfinished question" — restatement, opening]
[his: company of 500 people — R-13]
[his: "I approved the spend, settled the disagreement, decided what came next"]
[derived: the same instinct becomes the ceiling — compression of his own position]
[derived: the company moves at the speed of one person's calendar]
[derived: the question is the hinge, 72% depth]
[derived: telling the two apart is the real job — compression]
```

## 5. Worked example (illustrative only — not a Puneet post)

His one-liner (Thursday prompt, verbatim):
> "We hired someone who said no to me in the first meeting and it was the best signal I got
> all year."

Result (≈105 words):

> Most interviews measure how comfortable a candidate is saying yes. `[derived]`
>
> We hired someone who said no to me in the first meeting. It was the best signal I got that
> year. `[his]`
>
> Because agreeing is easy to rehearse. Saying no to the person paying you is not. `[derived]`
>
> I have watched teams read a yes as commitment and then spend a quarter finding out it was only
> politeness. `[derived]`
>
> Which is worth more to you, a candidate who agrees with you, or one who tells you when you are
> wrong? `[derived]`

**What did not happen:** no candidate described, no company named, no detail invented, no softer
version of "agreeing is easy to rehearse" that turns into a claim about candidates in general. The
insight is compressed from his own claim; the mechanism is a question, not an assertion.
